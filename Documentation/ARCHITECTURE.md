# Architecture - Materials and Challenges 0.10.0

The [v0.10 development plan](DEVELOPMENT_PLAN_V10.md) records this revision's acceptance criteria. Versioned delivery reports record executed validation, artifact metadata and device limits. Core swipe/camera/force tuning remains the v9 implementation.

GameBootstrap owns services, course loading/cancellation, mode selection and presentation. GameSession retains the guarded adventure lifecycle; RaceManager owns countdown/racing/pause/results separately. Authored route metadata no longer determines human controls or default camera heading.

## Input, physics and camera

BallInput samples touch/mouse/keyboard during Update and buffers finite displacement-derived force-time for one FixedUpdate consumer. Base gain is 0.65 and each deliberate stroke supplies at most 0.09 force-seconds, so repetition builds speed gradually. Responsive swipe uses restrained 0.85-1.15 speed gain; precision swipe uses constant gain. Both require actual travel, normalize by the smaller screen dimension, reject jitter and never replay held input. UI-started touches retain ownership. A stable primary finger drives throughout the non-UI screen; extra fingers never orbit or reset its anchor. Left/right mouse drags both move the ball. CameraGesture/CameraDelta remain zero compatibility APIs. Motion resumed after 110 ms adopts the current camera basis.

BallController latches the human camera frame for each stroke or keyboard hold and projects it onto support; external AI explicitly chooses PathForward and the normalized BallControlCommand contract. Camera rotation cannot bend an ongoing stroke. It applies symmetric force, grounded rolling torque, bounded slip correction, resistance and gravity. Standard drive is 27 force units, counter braking 32, rolling/viscous resistance 0.55 + 0.32 times speed. Human drive consumes its finite budget directly; AI retains 18 ms smoothing. A sampled burst is bounded using predicted powered velocity through force, never a velocity assignment. Pose/velocity writes are limited to explicit spawn/recovery and test setup.

BallProfile exposes force/speed, friction, braking, resistance, torque, air response and angular limits. ManualGameplayUpgradeGenerator applies new serialized defaults once through catalog.manualControlVersion. Legacy reverse/lateral fields remain compatible but do not impose asymmetric limits. CoursePathFollower reports adventure progress without setting player direction. RaceDriver uses mass-aware force feedback and resistance/slope compensation; its AI-only alternate-route queued impulse was removed.

BallGroundDetector combines contacts/nonalloc probes relative to MovementUp, rejects rivals as support, and exposes collider/point/body velocity. SurfaceContactResponse reads authored resistance independently of sound. BallController applies one GravityVector with built-in gravity disabled, preventing double gravity. Gravity Lab changes acceleration inside a bounded visible field; control/camera frames parallel-transport through floor/wall/ceiling orientation changes.

QuestCamera defaults to AdaptiveChase, follows position independently of sphere spin and ignores route heading. Travel above 0.35 m/s starts a bounded smooth heading response in the current camera update, with no angular gate or delay. Translation anchors to the interpolated ball and final optical composition fixes viewport x=0.5/y=0.4. Heading/gravity-up/obstruction arm are smoothed, including reverse. Normal gameplay always applies AdaptiveChase, and normal settings expose automatic follow. No human pointer gesture maps to camera orbit. Internal Orbit/StableOrbit APIs remain only for diagnostics and retained fixtures. Lens clipping is 0.25-350 m. Geometry probes and optional collision/landing/FOV effects remain.

QuestAudio owns music/cues and binds BallContactAudio to the player. Contact audio reads physical speed, slip, ground and impulse. Six revision-10 profiles reference 66 clips prepared from five credited CC0 recordings: three body clips each and eight paired body/surface impacts each. The selected ball retains its timbre on every surface; actual struck-collider identity selects the pair. Low-energy seam transitions are suppressed and slip is quieter. Two rolling voices, one slip voice and four impact voices remain bounded. Rest/airborne silence loops; mute/pause/deactivation stop existing audio. BallContactFeedback owns six surface-normal ripples and one 32-particle system outside the physics-ball hierarchy. [CONTACT_AUDIO_V10.md](CONTACT_AUDIO_V10.md) records source processing and audition limits.

LevelBuilder retains complete padded collision roads while RoadRenderGeometry removes duplicate coplanar visible patches. Flat deck normals, local road UVs, mip/aniso filtering and filtered two-cascade shadows reduce moving surface shimmer. Gravity Lab draws a continuous loop over its physical supports. Gate headers clear the chase camera; CourseSignVisibility fades nearby world labels. After legacy generators, CrazyCampaignGenerator contentVersion24 replaces all 55 campaign layouts in place, preserving IDs/GUIDs and five race definitions. Eleven interleaved spatial families have five individually authored route bodies each; normalized shape tests reject translated/scaled duplicates. CrazyTrackArtGenerator bakes ceramic/alloy/basalt maps; LevelBuilder selects family-specific visible finishes and contact identities without replacing validated collision geometry. CrazyCoursePresentation writes family-specific non-colliding landmarks into the existing static batches. SignatureAdventureGenerator separately seeds expedition assets IDs 56-60; version 3 includes the tunnel-safe Himalayan rejoin grade. ManualRaceCourseGenerator applies manualRaceVersion1 to the Canyon Choice fork. Featured completion does not bypass campaign unlocks.

SignatureCourseRuntime adds maze landmarks, mechanical structures, gravity road, mountain tunnel/scenery and volcanic environment. ClockworkMotor moves real kinematic supports/obstacles and implements ICourseResettable. SignatureGravityField runs before support detection. SignatureRuntimeResources disposes generated assets; static decoration batches separately from moving bodies.

The independent PhysicsLab scene has twelve stations. PhysicsLabTelemetry reads AppliedControl, actual body velocity/spin/contact, rolling ratio and coast distance, shows 10 Hz readouts and exports CSV. Reset clears external control, gravity and momentum. Main/lab scenes are in build lists, with a return-to-game control.

## UI, racing and saves

Main PLAY continues the campaign directly. CourseRouteGraphic draws actual route footprints. Completing 55 returns to the challenge list; featured adventures remain separately accessible. Existing native previews, rewards/shop/mission and racing flows remain. Race rank uses ordered 3D arcs/gates and validated crossings/DNF.

SaveData v10 adds the free Steel/Rubber styles and preserves balances, skins, campaign IDs and race records while selecting responsive swipe defaults and reducing prior sensitivity above 1 once. Later explicit swipe-mode/sensitivity choices persist, while normal camera follow is always automatic. Existing stars and best times remain; old best times measure earlier route geometry. Active cup points remain in memory: round results/rewards persist, but process death does not resume an unfinished cup. Daily rewards use device-UTC offline logic.

## Presentation ownership and performance

PremiumTrackPresentation writes round rails, deck fascia, supports, islands, clouds and bevelled towers directly into spatial material batches. PremiumTrackArtGenerator bakes reusable maple/window/water textures. CourseRenderBatcher consolidates the static architecture and signature-decoration roots; collision solids and mesh-filter references remain intact. Disabled renderers, text, transparent materials and Rigidbody hierarchies are excluded. These calls intentionally do not batch the whole course: rewards and animated pads live outside those roots.

PremiumCollectibleVisuals owns seven meshes and ten materials per course. Coins have two rendered parts with bevelled rims and correctly sloped embossed stars. Booster assemblies have three renderers and a shared animated shader: continuous dark housings, three raised champagne-alloy chevron plates, curved fins and recessed cyan/amber/jade lights. Activation lift/pulses are visual only. One loop updates cosmetic transforms, throttles distant animation and clears the near-camera view without moving or disabling reward triggers. Teardown releases owned meshes/materials. EffectPool reuses twelve particle slots and twelve expanding-ring slots.

BallLook shares shell meshes across all players and previews, using four mesh renderers per ball. Skin textures are cached by definition. Live menu previews render at 30 Hz; numeric adventure/race HUD text updates at 10 Hz. SRP batching is enabled. Non-development Android testing builds remove development instrumentation while retaining a debug signing certificate. Editor profiling and isolated managed-allocation checks are distinct from sustained phone performance.

The featured menu uses five native-rendered PNG previews under Resources/ExpeditionPreviews. Regression coverage includes all fifteen expedition variants, 55-course traversal, racing integrations, pointer sampling, full camera turnarounds and moving-junction rendering. Latest execution results are recorded in the delivery report; reruns do not add unique tests.


SculptedObstaclePresentation uses object-local material batches for small moving machinery, avoiding origin-based chunk splitting; scenery keeps spatial culling. QuestObstacle adds piston, sweep and drum motion and lifts timed-gate visuals independently of the physical root. CrazyCoursePresentation contains eleven distinct biome landmark recipes. The challenge precision pass narrows exposed roads, removes selected rails and places 69 new moving hazards; safe entries/drop catches and stable IDs remain.
