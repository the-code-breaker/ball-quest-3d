## What changed

The campaign now contains 55 individually authored route bodies across 11 interleaved families. Adjacent levels change spatial mechanics instead of replaying the same road with a different obstacle. Curves, long coils, returning hairpins, separated figure eights, lower catch roads, timed machinery, exposed catwalks, forks, boost/braking platforms and combined gauntlets replace the prior recipe. Fifty-one courses contain rail-free precision sections, 44 are marked hard/expert, and roads narrow to 1.65 metres. These design labels do not prove how difficult human players will find them.

Main **PLAY** now continues the campaign directly; it previously opened only the five featured adventures, making normal progression appear repetitive. Those adventures remain separately accessible. Level cards show actual route silhouettes, names and difficulty; a short pre-play briefing presents the selected route. Family-specific native scenery and ceramic, alloy, basalt or wood deck finishes distinguish courses visually. [Campaign design and route atlas](CRAZY_CAMPAIGN_V9.md).

All finger gestures control the ball. A stable primary finger can swipe forward, backward, sideways or around; adding another finger cannot orbit the camera or reset the primary anchor. Left/right mouse drags also move the ball. The camera automatically follows physical travel and reverse turns while preserving central framing. The v0.8 finite stroke budget remains: repeated swipes gradually add speed and holding still supplies no new drive. Ongoing strokes retain their captured world basis while the camera turns. Normal settings no longer expose fixed/manual orbit. [Controls](MANUAL_CONTROLS.md).

Original filtered friction noise and irregular contact grains replace the sustained motor-like rolling tone. Speed mainly affects loudness with restrained pitch variation. Metal, wood, glass, rubber, stone and energy balls have separate profiles, plus surface-specific impacts and selected rolling textures. The actual collider struck selects the impact material, independently of the supporting floor. Six pooled surface-normal ripples and one bounded particle system add contact animation without changing the ball's collision shape, force or scale; muted collisions still animate. The boost cue is a short noise whoosh. [Contact Foley and audition](CONTACT_AUDIO_V9.md).

Boosters use shared native geometry: recessed dark housings, three raised pearl-alloy direction plates, curved side fins and cyan/amber/jade flow lighting for acceleration, launch and conveyor variants. Activation pulses are cosmetic and leave collision/trigger behavior intact. Each pad retains three renderers and shared course resources. [Booster presentation and native evidence](BOOSTER_PRESENTATION_V9.md).

Existing currency, skins, unlocks, stars and records are retained by ID. Historical best times refer to the previous layouts and are not comparable to rebuilt routes. Five dedicated race definitions and five signature adventures remain; campaign rebuilding does not rewrite them. Save version 9 selects responsive defaults once and caps an old sensitivity above 1 at 1. Normal camera follow is always automatic.

## Art references and practical limits

[Generated level and booster concepts, saved image paths and exact prompts](ArtDirection/V9/README.md) document the image-generation work. Official publisher/store images were reviewed for swipe-driven play, elevated balance roads, ramps, gaps and obstacles. Proprietary game art/audio is not shipped. The generated concepts are illustration references; native gameplay captures show the actual original stylized geometry and materials.

Editor tests establish physical solvability, input/camera state, signal properties and bounded effects. They do not establish phone touch comfort, subjective difficulty, a perceptual sound match to another game, hardware-specific shimmer or sustained thermal/frame performance. The Android testing package is ARM64/IL2CPP/OpenGLES3, minimum API 26/target 36, debug-signed without development instrumentation. The [actual certificate comparison](../TestResults/signing-compatibility-v9.json) matches v0.8 and the version code advances to 9, supporting an update expected to preserve application data. Physical installation/update was not tested: the [final ADB check](../TestResults/adb-devices-v9.txt) found no attached phone. Phone touch comfort, audio preference/speaker output, hardware-specific rendering and sustained thermal/frame behavior remain unverified. Store signing/distribution and iOS remain separate from this completed Android testing build.
