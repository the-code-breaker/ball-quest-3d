# Recorded material contact — v0.10

The rolling bed now comes from CC0 recordings of actual metal and rubber balls instead of the v9 synthetic friction noise. Metal rolling on wood is credited to slamsal, rubber rolling to 89o_dev, rubber bouncing to SomeoneCool15, metal impact to RyanKingArt, and wood impact to Ian_G. [Sources, licenses and retained originals](References/V10/AUDIO_CREDITS.md).

The preparation script performs mono resampling to 22.05 kHz, band limiting, contact segment selection, dynamic normalization and head/tail overlap. Whole repeat periods preserve continuity: rolling loops last about 3.33 or 3.66 seconds. Source boundary jumps range from zero to 0.00617, below a 0.02 absolute acceptance limit and near ordinary adjacent-sample changes. This addresses clicks caused by cutting a repeated waveform at an unrelated sample; it is not a subjective listening result. [Seam measurements](../TestResults/natural-loop-seams-v10.json).

Six profiles each reference their own rolling, slip and body impact plus eight paired surface impacts. These **66 distinct clips** preserve the ball's material on wood, metal, stone, ice, sand, glass, grass and boost surfaces. Unlike v9, every ball no longer uses the same surface-only collision sample. Glass, stone and energy are processed interpretations of the recorded sources, not claims of recordings of those exact balls. The rolling loop retains the ball identity, with surface gain/pitch adjustments. [Processing script](../Tools/PrepareNaturalAudioV10.py), [clip metrics/source hashes](../TestResults/natural-contact-v10.json).

The slip layer is reduced from 0.16 to 0.055, with a higher slip threshold. Most rolling beds are also quieter in actual sample energy than the former synthetic bed. Speed mainly affects loudness and pitch remains constrained. Real collisions select the collider actually struck. Low-energy contact transitions between road meshes no longer create repeated impact audio or ripple events unless normal approach speed/impulse indicates a strike. Stationary, airborne, muted and paused balls remain silent. No audio routine applies a force, changes velocity, scales the collider or alters physical friction.

Brushed Steel, Copper Core, Carbon Rubber and Turned Walnut explicitly select metal, metal, rubber and wood sound identities. The collection labels the sound material. Steel and Rubber are available without spending coins; a v9 save keeps its currency, unlocks, selected owned skin and sensitivity while receiving them.

Four reusable impact voices, two crossfading rolling voices, one slip voice and bounded contact VFX retain the previous resource limits. The new profiles keep their original serialized GUIDs and migrate once at audio revision 10. Editor generation imports already prepared recordings; no network or audio generation runs on the phone. The old source generator remains a compatibility fallback, but its noisy WAV assets are excluded from the verified APK.

The dry [audition WAV](../TestResults/Audio/NaturalContactV10-Audition.wav) plays two seconds of each material's rolling followed by its body impact, separated by brief gaps, in order Metal, Wood, Glass, Rubber, Stone, Energy. It is source audio without music. Mathematical checks, resource checks and actual collision tests do not establish phone speaker output, human preference, or an exact audio match to another game. Final executed results are recorded in [DELIVERY_V10.md](DELIVERY_V10.md).
